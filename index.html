ADMIN MODECase 5/5
“Since we can't have everything, let's just drop that requirement and do this one instead.”

Back in Discovery, the client's PM weighed up two possible requirements, A and B, and after discussion formally chose Requirement A. This was written into the signed BRD, and Requirement B was parked. Fast forward to now, close to UAT, Requirement A has been fully built and tested. The same PM calls, friendly:

“Since we can't have everything, let's just drop Requirement A and do Requirement B instead. Straight swap only mah, one out one in, shouldn't cost anything extra right? Actually might even be faster.”

You check the decision log from discovery and confirm A was formally chosen and signed off, with B parked. You also check the build log and confirm A is fully built and tested.

Step 1 · Ask the right questions

Tick up to 2 questions you would ask, then select “Ask these questions” to lock them in.

☐Is there a hard deadline driving why B needs to come in now?
☐Just to check, is Requirement A no longer needed going forward, or is this more about wanting B in first and coming back to A later?
☐I remember we confirmed A together back in discovery, can I ask what's changed since then that's making B more of a priority now?
☐Didn't we already spend a lot of effort building A? Why would you want to throw that away?
☐Why do you suddenly want to swap now after already confirming A?
☐Would B need to connect with anything we've already built for A, or would it stand on its own?

You can pick up to 2 questions, 2 left.

Step 2 · Name it (10 pts)

Which form of scope creep is this?

Ask your Step 1 questions first to unlock this step.

📋More Of What's Already There
Beyond what we signed, but similar to what's already in there.
🆕A Brand-New Requirement
Something that isn't in the BRD at all.
🔄A Change To What Was Agreed
The requirement stays, but the design or logic shifts after sign-off.
⏱️A Change In Timeline
A phase moves or compresses, or scope is swapped or dropped.

Step 3 · Pick your response (up to 10 pts)

Each option includes what happens next, read the whole thing before you choose.

“A straight swap does sound fair, and I can see the logic there. I'll log this as a change on our side, and since we're dropping A, I'll factor that into the costing for B.”Next step: Logs the change, nets A's spent effort against B's cost in the estimate.
“A straight swap does sound fair, and since it's the same overall scope, happy to make that change for you. I'll just make sure B is properly built and tested before we reach go live.”Next step: Updates the plan, proceeds with building B.
“A straight swap does sound fair, and I get where you're coming from. Just to flag, we confirmed A together back in discovery and it's already built and tested, while B would be a fresh build. Let me work out the real cost, then we can discuss.”Next step: Logs as a change request, works out true cost with project lead, discusses before agreeing.
